How to Reach Live Chat

Live chat is normally reachable from a chat icon or "Support" link once you're logged into your account, and often from the logged-out site as well for pre-registration questions. Open the chat window, describe your issue in a short, clear sentence, and an agent picks it up from there. When to Use Email Instead

Live chat is the faster option for anything urgent — a stuck transaction, a login problem, a question before you deposit. Email makes more sense when:

  • You need to attach a document (for example, ID verification or a payment receipt).
  • The issue is complex enough that a written trail helps — a dispute over a bonus term, or a withdrawal that's taking longer than expected.
  • You'd rather have a timestamped record of exactly what was said, for your own reference.

Send a clear subject line and your registered account email in the body, since support will need that to look up your account regardless of which address you write from. It's also worth sending important account changes — like an updated payment detail or a formal complaint — by email even if you've already discussed them in live chat, simply because a chat window is easy to lose track of once it closes, while an email sits in your sent folder indefinitely.

What to Have Ready Before Contacting Support

A few details ready up front turn a slow back-and-forth into a single exchange:

  1. Your registered account email — the one you signed up with, not necessarily the one you're writing from.
  2. A specific description of the issue — "my deposit hasn't credited" is more useful than "something's wrong," and including the approximate time and amount helps an agent locate the transaction fast.
  3. Screenshots, if relevant — a stuck payment screen or an error message is far easier to diagnose visually than described in words.
  4. What you've already tried — if you've already refreshed, logged out and back in, or checked your bank app, say so, so the agent doesn't send you through the same first-line steps again.

Response Expectations — Honestly

We'd rather tell you plainly what we don't know than invent a reassuring number. Longfu88 doesn't publish a specific response-time target for either live chat or email on the official site, so any claim you see elsewhere quoting an exact figure — "replies in under 60 seconds," for instance — isn't something we can verify and we won't repeat it here. What we can say based on the nature of each channel: live chat is a real-time conversation by design, so expect to be talking to an agent within the session rather than waiting on a callback, while email is inherently a queued channel and may take longer to see a reply, particularly outside of the busiest periods. If a matter is time-sensitive, live chat is the better starting point. We'd rather set that honest expectation than let you assume email will behave like an instant channel and then feel let down when it doesn't.

Escalating an Unresolved Issue

If live chat can't resolve something on the first attempt — a bonus dispute that needs a second look, or a withdrawal that's genuinely stuck — a few steps help move things forward:

  1. Ask the agent to escalate or flag the case, and request a reference or ticket number if one is offered. Having something to point back to makes a follow-up conversation faster.
  2. Follow up by email using the reference if you have one, laying out the issue and the chat history in writing. This creates a record that a fresh live-chat conversation each time doesn't.
  3. Be specific and factual in any escalation — dates, amounts, and what you were told previously. Vague follow-ups tend to restart the process rather than move it forward.
  4. Keep your own records — screenshots of chat transcripts and confirmation emails — from the start of any issue that looks like it might need more than one contact to resolve.
