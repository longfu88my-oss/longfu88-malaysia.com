Football is the sport Malaysian bettors follow most closely, and it's also the sport with the widest range of betting markets — which is exactly why it's worth learning the basics properly before staking real money. This guide covers the core bet types, how decimal odds actually pay out with a worked ringgit example, the difference between a single and a parlay, and how to stake sensibly, using the Longfu88 Sportsbook as the reference point throughout.

The Core Bet Types

  • 1X2 (Match Result) — a straight bet on home win, draw or away win, with no adjustment. The most straightforward market to read.
  • Asian Handicap (AH) — one side gets a virtual head start or deficit to level a mismatched contest, which removes the draw as a pure loss outcome for favourites-backers. See the full mechanics and worked examples in our Asian Handicap guide.
  • Over/Under Total Goals — a bet on whether the combined goals scored in the match will land over or under a set line, commonly 2.5 or 3.5 goals.
  • Both Teams to Score (BTTS) — a yes/no market on whether both sides score at least once, independent of the final result.
  • Correct Score — predicting the exact final scoreline. The highest-risk, highest-reward market of the core five, since there are many possible outcomes and only one is correct.

Most of these markets are listed side by side for every fixture on the Longfu88 Sportsbook, so you can compare how a straight match-result price differs from an Asian Handicap or BTTS line on the same game before deciding where the value looks best to you.

Decimal Odds & How Payouts Work

Odds are shown in decimal format on the Longfu88 Sportsbook, which is the most straightforward style to calculate without a betting calculator. The number shown represents your total return per ringgit staked — your original stake included — so your payout is simply your stake multiplied by the odds, and your profit is that payout minus your stake.

Worked example. Say a BTTS "yes" market shows odds of 1.75 (an illustrative price, not a quote tied to any real fixture) and you stake MYR 40. Your total return if both teams score is MYR 40 × 1.75 = MYR 70, meaning MYR 30 is profit and MYR 40 is your stake returned. A shorter price — say 1.30 on a strong favourite in a match-result market — with the same MYR 40 stake would return MYR 52 total, just MYR 12 profit, reflecting how a more likely outcome pays proportionally less.

Single Bets vs Parlays (Accumulators)

A single bet is one selection on one market, settled entirely on its own — win or lose, nothing else affects it. A parlay, also called an accumulator, combines several selections into one ticket, where the odds of every leg multiply together for a larger combined price, but every single leg has to win for the ticket to pay out at all. A four-leg parlay with modest odds on each leg can produce a much bigger headline return than a single bet — but losing any one of the four legs loses the entire stake, even if the other three all won. For that reason, singles are generally the more disciplined way to start: they isolate risk to one outcome at a time, while a parlay concentrates several independent risks into a single point of failure.

Parlay worked example. Say you combine three legs at illustrative odds of 1.80, 1.60 and 2.00 into one parlay ticket. The combined odds are the three numbers multiplied together: 1.80 × 1.60 × 2.00 = 5.76. Stake MYR 20 on that ticket and, if all three legs win, your total return is MYR 20 × 5.76 = MYR 115.20 (MYR 95.20 profit). But if just one of the three legs loses — even if the other two would have won on their own as singles — the entire MYR 20 stake is lost, which is the core risk trade-off a parlay makes for its bigger headline price.

Staking & Bankroll

Treat your betting bankroll as a fixed pool separate from day-to-day money, and stake a consistent, modest percentage of it per bet rather than varying your stake based on how confident a particular pick feels in the moment. Chasing a losing run by increasing stakes to "get back to even" is one of the most common ways a manageable bankroll gets wiped out quickly — a fixed unit size protects against that instinct better than willpower alone. Set a session or weekly loss limit before you start betting, not after a rough patch, and stop once you hit it.

Common Questions

How do decimal odds work?＋

Decimal odds show your total return per ringgit staked, stake included. Multiply your stake by the decimal number to get your full payout, then subtract your original stake to see the profit — MYR 50 at odds of 2.50 returns MYR 125 total, MYR 75 of which is profit.

What is BTTS?＋

BTTS stands for Both Teams To Score — a yes/no market on whether both sides find the net at least once during the match, completely independent of who wins, loses or draws. A 2-1 or 1-1 scoreline settles BTTS as 'yes'; a 1-0 or 0-0 scoreline settles it as 'no'.

Should I bet singles or parlays (accumulators)?＋

A single is one bet on one outcome, settled on its own. A parlay (accumulator) bundles several selections into one bet, where every leg must win for the whole ticket to pay — the odds multiply together for a bigger potential return, but any single losing leg loses the entire ticket. Singles are generally the more disciplined starting point; parlays carry meaningfully higher risk for the higher reward.

What's the minimum bet on football markets?＋

Minimum stakes vary by market and fixture rather than being fixed sitewide — the exact figure is shown on the bet slip before you confirm. Check the slip for the specific market you're betting rather than assuming one minimum applies across every football market.
