What "P2P" (Player-vs-Player) Means

P2P stands for player-vs-player. Instead of betting against the house's fixed odds — the way you would in live baccarat or blackjack — you're competing directly against other real players in the same round. The platform and the provider's software host the table, deal the cards or roll the dice, and enforce the rules, but the winner is determined by how the players' hands or bets compare to each other, not by a dealer representing the house. It's the same underlying idea as a home card game formalised into a licensed, software-run format: real competition between players, run through a digital table instead of a physical one.

The Four P2P Categories

  • Table Games — familiar table formats reworked into a player-vs-player structure.
  • Dice — dice-based rounds where players' outcomes are compared against each other rather than the house.
  • Casual — lighter, faster-paced formats aimed at quick rounds.

How P2P Differs From Live Casino

The clearest way to separate the two: in live casino, you're betting against the house's odds on an outcome dealt by a real dealer streamed on video — the house sets the payout structure (like baccarat's 5% Banker commission), and every player at that table is betting independently against the same dealer. In P2P, there's no dealer representing the house at all — the software runs the mechanics, but your win or loss is determined relative to the other players in that round. A live baccarat table and a P2P baccarat table can share a name and basic card values, but the competitive structure underneath is fundamentally different. How P2P Differs From Fast Games

This is the pairing that trips up the most players, because both categories move quickly: Fast Games — Crash, Mines, Plinko, Crab — are single-round games you play against fixed game mechanics, with a result generated independently for your bet alone, closer in structure to a slot than to a card table. P2P, by contrast, always involves other players' hands or bets as part of how your round resolves. Speed is not what separates the two categories; the presence or absence of other competing players is. Never treat a P2P result as if it worked like a Fast Game's independent round, or vice versa — the mechanics genuinely differ, not just the pacing.

A Simple Way to Picture the Stakes

P2P rounds work on a shared-pot or head-to-head basis rather than a fixed house payout table, so the exact structure varies by title — a Teen Patti round, for instance, typically has each active player contribute a stake into the round, with the strongest hand at showdown taking the result. Say you and three other players each put MYR 10 into a Teen Patti round: the combined stakes in play total MYR 40 across the table, and how that resolves between the players depends on the specific title's rules for betting rounds, folding and showdown — mechanics that differ enough between Teen Patti, Andar Bahar and 32 Cards that it's worth reading a specific title's in-game rules before your first round rather than assuming one card game's structure carries over to another. Because your result depends on other players' decisions as well as your own hand, bankroll management in P2P is less about bet-sizing formulas and more about knowing when a hand is worth continuing to contest versus folding early.
