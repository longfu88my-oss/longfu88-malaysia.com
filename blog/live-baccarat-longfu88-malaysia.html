What Live Baccarat Is at Longfu88 Malaysia

Live baccarat streams a real dealer, a real shoe of cards and a real table to your screen, with betting placed through the Longfu88 interface rather than at a physical rail. You're watching the same shuffle, the same deal and the same reveal as a player sitting in the studio — the software just handles your chips. Two hands are dealt each round, called Banker and Player (neither belongs to the house or to you personally — they're simply the two sides of the table), and your job is to guess which hand finishes closer to 9, or whether the two hands tie. Face cards and 10s count as zero, aces count as one, and any total over 9 drops its tens digit — a hand of 8 and 7 is worth 5, not 15. There's no strategy to learn beyond which bet to place; the drawing rules that decide whether a third card is dealt are fixed and handled automatically by the dealer.

The Three Bets and the Odds

Every round offers exactly three ways to bet:

  • Banker — wins slightly more often than Player across a full shoe, and pays even money minus a 5% commission on the win.
  • Player — wins slightly less often than Banker, and pays straight even money with no commission.
  • Tie — both hands finish on the same total, typically paying 8 to 1 (some tables pay 9 to 1 — check the paytable on the specific table before betting, since it varies by studio).

Because Banker wins marginally more often once the drawing rules are factored in, its house edge after commission works out to roughly 1.06% — the best value of the three. Player sits close behind at around 1.24%. Tie is the outlier: it pays big when it hits, but it hits rarely enough that its house edge runs close to 14%, which is why most experienced players treat it as a side novelty rather than a core bet.

Why There's a 5% Commission on Banker

The commission exists because Banker's third-card drawing rules give it a small structural edge over Player — left uncorrected, Banker would win often enough to make the bet too favourable to offer. Casinos balance this by taking 5% off Banker's winnings only, which brings its edge back roughly in line with Player's. The commission applies solely to wins; a losing Banker bet costs you nothing extra, and a tied hand doesn't trigger it either, since the bet simply pushes.

Three Worked Examples in MYR

Example 1 — Banker bet, Banker wins. You stake MYR 100 on Banker and the hand wins. You're paid even money minus 5% commission: MYR 100 × 0.95 = MYR 95 profit. The table returns your MYR 100 stake plus MYR 95, for MYR 195 total back.

Example 2 — Player bet, Player wins. You stake MYR 50 on Player and the hand wins. No commission applies, so you win straight even money: MYR 50 profit, MYR 100 returned in total.

Example 3 — Tie bet, and a Banker/Player push. You stake MYR 20 on Tie and the two hands finish level. At 8 to 1, that's MYR 160 profit, MYR 180 returned. Now picture a friend at the same table who staked MYR 50 on Banker instead of Tie — because the hand tied rather than Banker or Player winning outright, their Banker bet neither wins nor loses. It pushes, and the dealer simply hands back their MYR 50 stake untouched.

No-Commission Tables: What Changes

Some studios run a "no commission" or "commission-free" variant of baccarat, where Banker pays straight even money like Player does — with one adjustment to keep the house edge intact, most commonly that a winning Banker hand totalling exactly 6 pays only half (0.5 to 1) instead of even money. The rules are always displayed on the table before you sit down, since the payout structure changes even though the cards and drawing rules don't.
