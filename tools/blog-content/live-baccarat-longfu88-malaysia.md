Face cards and 10s count as zero, aces count as one, and any total over 9 drops its tens digit — a hand of 8 and 7 is worth 5, not 15. There's no strategy to learn beyond which bet to place; the drawing rules that decide whether a third card is dealt are fixed and handled automatically by the dealer.

## The Three Bets and the Odds

Every round offers exactly three ways to bet:

- **Banker** — wins slightly more often than Player across a full shoe, and pays even money minus a 5% commission on the win.
- **Player** — wins slightly less often than Banker, and pays straight even money with no commission.
- **Tie** — both hands finish on the same total, typically paying 8 to 1 (some tables pay 9 to 1 — check the paytable on the specific table before betting, since it varies by studio).

Because Banker wins marginally more often once the drawing rules are factored in, its house edge after commission works out to roughly 1.06% — the best value of the three. Player sits close behind at around 1.24%. Tie is the outlier: it pays big when it hits, but it hits rarely enough that its house edge runs close to 14%, which is why most experienced players treat it as a side novelty rather than a core bet.

## Why There's a 5% Commission on Banker

The commission exists because Banker's third-card drawing rules give it a small structural edge over Player — left uncorrected, Banker would win often enough to make the bet too favourable to offer. Casinos balance this by taking 5% off Banker's winnings only, which brings its edge back roughly in line with Player's. The commission applies solely to wins; a losing Banker bet costs you nothing extra, and a tied hand doesn't trigger it either, since the bet simply pushes.
