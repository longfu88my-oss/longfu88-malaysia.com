The Premier League is the most-watched football league among Malaysian sports bettors, and the new season restarts the whole cycle — fresh form, fresh table, fresh markets to learn or relearn. This guide covers what to expect from the timing of the 2026/27 campaign, the markets that see the most action, and how to read MYR odds properly before you place a bet, without pretending to know how any specific fixture will play out.

## Season Overview: What to Expect From 2026/27

The English Premier League's 2026/27 season kicks off in **mid-August 2026**, running across 38 match rounds per club through to May the following year, as is standard for the competition's format — 20 clubs, home-and-away fixtures against every other side. This guide won't speculate on transfers, squad news or predicted results — team news and form only become meaningful once matches are actually played, and any "trends" claimed before a ball is kicked are guesswork dressed up as analysis. What's useful to know in advance is simply the calendar: the season runs almost the full year, meaning there's a long runway of weekly fixtures rather than a short tournament window, which changes how you should think about bankroll planning compared to a short knockout event.

## Popular Markets for EPL Betting

A handful of markets account for most of the betting volume on any given EPL round:

- **Match Result (1X2)** — a straight bet on home win, draw or away win. The simplest market to read, with no handicap adjustment.
- **Asian Handicap (AH)** — one team gets a virtual head start or deficit to level a mismatched contest, removing the draw as a pure loss outcome for favourites-backers. See our [Asian Handicap explained guide](/blog/asian-handicap-explained) for full mechanics and worked examples.
- **Over/Under Total Goals** — betting on whether the combined goals in a match will be over or under a set number, commonly lines like 2.5 or 3.5 goals.
- **Both Teams to Score (BTTS)** — a yes/no market on whether both sides find the net at least once, independent of who wins.

Most sportsbooks, including the [Longfu88 Sportsbook](/sportsbook), list these markets side by side for every fixture, so you can compare a straight match-result price against an Asian Handicap line on the same match before deciding which offers better value for the way you read the game.

## Ante-Post & Long-Term Markets

Beyond weekly match markets, sportsbooks typically list **ante-post** (long-term, outright) markets that run for the whole season rather than a single fixture — outright title winner, top-4 finish, relegation and "will finish above/below" head-to-head markets between two clubs are the common examples. These markets settle only once the season concludes, which means your stake is tied up for months rather than resolved that weekend, and the odds move throughout the season as form changes. This guide won't name specific clubs as favourites for any of these markets before a ball is kicked — that kind of pre-season "certainty" is exactly the guesswork worth avoiding. If ante-post markets interest you, treat them as a small, separate portion of your bankroll rather than your primary way of betting the season, precisely because the money is locked up so much longer than a weekly bet.

## Reading Odds in MYR: A Worked Example

Odds on the Longfu88 Sportsbook are shown in decimal format, which is the easiest style to calculate by hand. The decimal number represents your total return per ringgit staked — stake included — so multiplying your stake by the odds gives your full payout, and subtracting your original stake gives your profit.

**Example.** Say a match-result market shows odds of 2.10 on the away side (a purely illustrative price, not a quote from any real fixture), and you stake MYR 80. Your total return if that outcome hits is MYR 80 × 2.10 = MYR 168, meaning MYR 88 of that is profit and MYR 80 is your original stake coming back. If the odds were shorter — say 1.50 on a heavy favourite — the same MYR 80 stake would return MYR 120 total (MYR 40 profit), reflecting how a more likely outcome pays less per ringgit risked.

## Staking & Bankroll Discipline Across a Long Season

A 38-round league season is a marathon, not a single-match sprint, and that changes how staking should be approached compared to a one-off cup final. Setting a fixed unit size — a consistent percentage of your bankroll per bet rather than varying stakes wildly based on how confident a particular pick feels — helps avoid the common trap of chasing a losing run with progressively larger bets. Treat each round as one data point in a long season rather than a must-win moment, and set a loss limit for the week or month that you're comfortable walking away at, regardless of how the fixtures are going.
