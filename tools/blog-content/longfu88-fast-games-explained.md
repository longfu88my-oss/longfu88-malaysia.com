## What Fast (Instant) Games Are

A Fast Game is a single-round, instant-resolution title — you place a bet, the round runs its course in a matter of seconds, and the result is final. There's no dealer to wait on, no other players' hands to factor in, and no multi-step betting structure like a live table's rounds. Mechanically, Fast Games sit closer to slots than to anything else in the lobby: the outcome is generated by the provider's RNG software for your bet specifically, independent of what any other player does at the same moment. What makes the category distinct is presentation and pacing — Crash-style rising multipliers, Mines-style grid reveals, Plinko-style physics drops — rather than a fundamentally different underlying mechanism from a slot spin.

## Crash, Mines, Plinko and Crab: How Each Works

Four mechanics make up the category, each represented by several branded titles:

- **Crash** (titles like Crash, CrashX, JetX, Spaceman, AERO and Football X) — a multiplier climbs from 1x upward for as long as the round runs, and you choose when to cash out before it "crashes" back to zero. Cash out too early and you bank a small win; wait too long and the round can end before you tap out.
- **Mines** (Mines, 1Tap Mines, Turbo Mines) — you reveal tiles on a grid one at a time, with each safe tile increasing your potential payout and a hidden mine ending the round if you hit it. You choose when to stop and collect rather than keep revealing.
- **Plinko** (Plinko, Turbo Plinko, Olympus Plinko) — a ball drops through a pegged board and lands in one of several slots at the bottom, each carrying a different multiplier, in a mechanic modelled on the classic drop-board game.
- **Crab** (Bonus Crab, Cash Crab) — a themed instant-round format with its own multiplier or reveal mechanic; specifics vary by title, so check the in-game rules for how a given Crab title resolves.

## Volatility and Bankroll: A Worked MYR Example

Fast Games can burn through a bankroll quickly simply because rounds resolve so fast — there's no dealer pace slowing down how many bets you place per hour. Say you sit down with a session bankroll of **MYR 150** and decide to bet **MYR 5** per round on a Crash-style title, aiming for a modest 1.5x cash-out target each time. That gives you roughly 30 rounds if every one lost, though in practice a mix of wins and losses will move your balance up and down throughout the session rather than declining in a straight line. If you win a round at 1.5x, you collect MYR 7.50 back on that MYR 5 stake (MYR 2.50 profit); if the round crashes before your cash-out point, you lose the MYR 5 stake outright. Deciding your per-round stake as a fixed small fraction of your total bankroll before you start — rather than increasing it after a loss to "catch up" — is the single most reliable way to make a bankroll last through a session's natural swings.

## Why Fast Games Differ From P2P

It's worth being precise here, because the two categories get confused: **Fast Games** resolve your bet against the game's own mechanics alone — no other player's hand, bet or decision affects your result. **P2P** titles, covered in our [P2P games guide](/blog/longfu88-p2p-games-explained), resolve based on how your hand or bet compares against other real players in the same round. A Crash multiplier climbing on your screen has nothing to do with what anyone else at Longfu88 is doing at that moment; a Teen Patti hand in P2P very much does. Treat them as entirely separate product categories, not variations of the same thing.

## Fast Games vs. Slots: A Quick Note

Fast Games and slots are mechanically closer to each other than either is to live casino or P2P — both resolve your bet independently through the provider's RNG software, with no dealer and no other player's hand involved. The difference is presentation and pacing rather than underlying structure: a slot's outcome is fixed the instant you press spin, while several Fast Games (Crash and Mines in particular) give you an in-round decision point — when to cash out, when to stop revealing — that a standard slot doesn't offer.
