Stay in control

How to Play Responsibly

A few simple habits keep gambling fun and stop it from taking over. Build these into every session and you will always be playing on your own terms.

Set a budget

Decide before you play how much money you can comfortably afford to lose, and never spend more than that. Only ever gamble with money set aside for entertainment — never funds meant for bills, rent or family.

Set time limits

Decide how long you will play and stick to it. Take regular breaks, and avoid long, unbroken sessions where you lose track of the clock.

Never chase losses

If you are down, accept it as the cost of the entertainment and stop. Trying to win back losses by betting more is one of the fastest ways for gambling to become a problem.

Don't play when upset

Avoid gambling when you are stressed, angry, anxious or low. Decisions made in a difficult mood are rarely good ones, and gambling will not fix how you feel.

Stay clear-headed

Don't gamble under the influence of alcohol or anything else that clouds your judgement. Play only when you can think clearly and make calm choices.

Keep life balanced

Make sure gambling is just one of many things you enjoy. Keep up with work, family, friends and hobbies so it never becomes the centre of your day.

Know the signs

Warning Signs of Problem Gambling

Problem gambling can build up gradually, and it is not always easy to spot in yourself. If several of the signs on the right feel familiar — for you or for someone you care about — it may be time to step back, use the player tools above, or reach out for support. Recognising the signs early makes them far easier to deal with.

None of these signs mean you have done anything wrong. They are simply a prompt to pause and check in. Talking to someone, whether that is a free helpline or a trusted friend, is a sign of strength, not weakness.

Warning signs
  • Spending more time or money than you planned
  • Chasing losses to win money back
  • Gambling with money needed for bills or family
  • Borrowing money or selling things to gamble
  • Hiding your gambling from people close to you
  • Feeling anxious, guilty or irritable about it
  • Neglecting work, study, family or friends
